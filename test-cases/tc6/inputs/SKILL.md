# Describe System

Own delivery of a coherent system description from source discovery through reader validation. Do not claim authority beyond the supplied evidence or the capabilities that establish it.

A reader may be any consumer of the description.

## Align proportionately

Before substantial work, propose the system boundary, source boundary, reader and use, and intended outcome in one short sentence and request confirmation. Infer a reasonable proposal from context rather than returning a questionnaire. Proceed directly when the work is already bounded and not substantial.

Retain ownership of user alignment throughout delegated work. Realign only when new evidence materially changes the confirmed boundary, use, authority, or outcome.

## Establish the model

1. Discover sources within the confirmed boundary and record what was inspected, included, excluded, or left uninspected, with the reason and stable source identity.
2. Map the system's identities, boundaries, elements, ports, relationships, directions, flows, behavior, states, and dependencies at the resolution required by the reader.
3. Attach provenance, authority, uncertainty, conflicts, omissions, and validity limits to the model content they affect.
4. Reconcile compatible evidence without collapsing unresolved differences or choosing unsupported governing truth.
5. Close coverage against the confirmed boundary or expose what prevents closure. Do not validate against a model whose source coverage is unknown.

Model construction may use another capability, but it remains an internal lane. Give it the confirmed boundary and sources; require a bounded model with stable identities, evidence links, unresolved conditions, and coverage. Do not expose a second alignment workflow to the reader.

## Compile the description

1. Establish what the reader must recover and any interface through which it will consume the result.
2. Select and organize the model content and dependencies necessary for that recovery.
3. Determine which additional representations are necessary and the authority each must carry.
4. Give each producer the relevant model identities, relationships, evidence, constraints, uncertainty, omissions, and required validation; require the same fields and model mappings in return.
5. Integrate every representation through stable model identities. Preserve its native authority and validation basis.
6. Maintain a coverage map from in-scope model content to its representation or explicit omission.

Do not omit model content merely to simplify a representation. Split or coordinate representations when one cannot remain complete and legible.

## Validate the compiled result

Validate independently of presentation quality:

- Trace material content from source to model to delivered representation.
- Check repeated facts and relationships for agreement and verify material source-internal consistency when the reader's use depends on it; obtain qualified validation when required authority is unavailable locally.
- Check every represented relationship for supported endpoints, direction, type, continuity across boundaries, and correct attachment. Trace every claimed control or feedback function through its complete functional path.
- Compare the coverage map with the confirmed model and account for every missing, invented, reversed, dangling, or misrouted element or relationship.
- Consume the actual delivered result through the reader's interface and exercise its required behavior and states. Producer success, source inspection, and self-report are not substitutes.
- Distinguish source defects from inspection or execution artifacts by checking through the applicable consumption environment and corroborating consequential findings.

If a required validation cannot run, mark the affected result and claim unverified rather than treating inspection as proof.

## Guards

- Do not invent unsupported system facts. Representational construction derived from the model is permitted but does not create new system authority.
- Do not repair missing knowledge through presentation.
- Do not let completeness, clarity, polish, precision, or behavior imply stronger authority than the evidence establishes.
- Do not prescribe a representation, production method, or delivery form independently of the confirmed reader and use.
- Do not absorb the authority of a source, model owner, producer, validator, or reader.
- When a request exceeds supported descriptive authority, complete the supported portion and expose the unsupported authority, evidence, owner, or capability needed.
- Do not execute active source content outside the confirmed trust and permission boundary.

## Deliver

Deliver the compiled description with its confirmed boundary, source coverage, model-to-representation coverage, validation evidence, and unresolved model, description, authority, or capability gaps.
